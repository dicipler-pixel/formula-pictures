Hamid Naderi Yeganeh, “View of a Black Hole from a Planet”

How the black-hole equation is built

Jeromie Beasley · 22 October 2026

The formula is a picture written as algebra: a stack of a few simple operations. Shapes are level sets. Edges are soft steps. Glows are bands around a shape. Ridges are folds. Texture is a fractal sum. Depth is an occlusion chain. Colour is a quadratic in the channel number. Below are three demonstrations: the formula is rendered exactly, taken apart term by term, and then the same grammar builds a new picture from scratch.

2. The grammar

Every term in the formula is one of these operations. The exact identities were checked numerically.

3. Taken apart

Switch off one group of terms and exactly one element of the picture disappears. Without the mountain layers you see the clouds that were behind them, so the depth order is real. Without the texture E, the clouds become smooth ovals: they are a blob pattern with fractal edges.

What this shows

Every term of the black-hole formula is one of eight simple operations, and each group of terms draws exactly one element of the picture. Run forwards, the same operations write a new formula for a new scene that renders exactly as designed. That is a working, checkable account of how equations like these are made: design the scene as shapes, edges, glows, folds, texture and layers; write each as its operation; then tune the constants by eye. His constants are round fractions such as 21/20, 3/10 and 9/10, which looks hand-tuned rather than fitted.

What it cannot show is his private process: the order he works in, or how many trials each constant took. Only he can say that.
